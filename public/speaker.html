Teamwork in a changing world

Communicate. Collaborate.
Make time to reflect.

Communicate with clarity.

Make expectations clear, ask better questions and give feedback people can use. When roles, tools and priorities are changing, good communication helps people find a shared direction.

Collaborate across strengths.

Understand what each person brings and how those strengths work together. Create the trust and motivation people need to contribute, learn from one another and take ownership.

Reflect. Learn. Adapt.

Make space to pause, challenge assumptions and learn from experience. Development means looking at people, teams and the systems around them, so useful changes can become part of everyday work.

Ideas that stay with you

Real research.
A fresh perspective.

Surprising stories, useful questions and research that connects with everyday leadership. Here is a taste of the ideas we can explore together.

4,000+

Pilots. No “average” person.

More than 4,000 US Air Force pilots were measured across ten body dimensions. Not one fell within the average range on all ten. Todd Rose uses this story to challenge designing for an imaginary average person. What changes when we build teams around real individuals?

Read Harvard’s account ↗

Up to29%

The business case for strengths.

A Gallup meta-analysis by Jim Asplund and colleagues estimated profit gains of 14.4–29.4% associated with strengths-based development. Drawing on 43 studies across 22 organisations and 1.2 million employees, it asks a compelling question: are you investing enough in what your people do best?

A few questions to take into your team.

What if there is no single scale for talent?

Todd Rose calls it jaggedness: our abilities and characteristics form an uneven profile. A single score or label can hide what someone brings. We explore how to recognise complementary strengths and create roles that help different people contribute.

The thinking behind jagged profiles ↗
Bee glasses or fly glasses: what are you looking for?

A metaphor from my strengths work: are you looking for the flowers or only noticing the mess? We explore how attention shapes feedback, relationships and the possibilities we see in people — while still addressing problems that need our attention.

Is giving everybody the same task always fair?

Imagine asking every animal to climb the same tree. The task is identical; the opportunity to excel is not. This illustration opens a conversation about fair expectations, different starting points and what meaningful contribution looks like in your team.

Do you know your strengths as well as a carpenter knows their tools?

A carpenter knows which tool suits the job. Leaders need a similar awareness of their own strengths: when they help, when they are overused and when somebody else’s approach is needed. Knowing your tools is the beginning; learning how to use them takes practice.

Training helps. Where should we invest it?

In their paper Investing in Strengths, Donald Clifton and James Harter describe Glock’s 1955 reading study: students who started as faster readers made the largest gains. It helped inspire a question central to strengths development: what happens when we invest in existing talent? We explore talent alongside practice, skills and knowledge, and the weaknesses that still need managing.

Keynote questions,
answered.

What are your keynote topics?

My core themes are intrinsic motivation, strengths orientation and positive leadership. Across these talks, the aim is to improve teamwork, communication and motivation, and help people, teams and systems develop.

Can you tailor a keynote to our organisation?

Yes. We start with your audience, your challenges and the conversation you want to open. I tailor the examples, emphasis and format around your needs, including how your teams collaborate, communicate and reflect through change.

Do you speak in English and German?

Yes. Keynotes are available in native English or fluent German. We agree the language and terminology around your audience during the briefing.

Can a talk address teamwork during AI-driven change?

Yes. We can explore the human questions that change brings: how people communicate, build trust, contribute their strengths and learn together. The focus is on leadership and team development, shaped around your organisation’s situation.

Can you combine a keynote with a discussion or workshop?

Yes. An optional discussion or facilitated session can help the audience connect the ideas with its own work. We agree the format and timing around your programme.
